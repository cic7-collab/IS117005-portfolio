# Research method

## Research question

Which AI-related and adjacent technology careers are projected or expected to grow, what work do these jobs involve, and how could a student prepare for them?

## Evidence used

1. **U.S. occupational projections:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Occupational Outlook Handbook reports projected employment changes for standardized occupations. This project uses the 2024–34 outlook where available.
2. **Global employer expectations:** The World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025* draws on a survey of employers and describes expected role changes through 2030. It is not a government count of job openings.
3. **Role descriptions:** BLS occupation pages and employer job listings help distinguish routine responsibilities and qualifications. Job listings should be collected with their date and location because requirements vary.

## Important limits

- “AI jobs” do not form one standardized occupation. AI work appears under data, software, research, security, and other titles.
- A projected growth percentage applies to an occupation and a period, not to each company or individual worker.
- Employer survey expectations are not the same as observed employment projections. The WEF global survey and U.S. BLS projections have different geography, methods, and time horizons.
- Skills and credentials vary by employer. A sample of listings can illustrate patterns but does not represent every employer.
- A fast-growing occupation can still be competitive and may have demanding entry requirements.

## Next research steps

- Collect a small, dated sample of current entry-level and internship postings for each career family.
- Code postings for tasks, required tools, education, experience, and location.
- Compare recurring requirements with academic courses and realistic portfolio projects.
- Revisit projections as new official data becomes available.

## Citation practice

Link claims to primary sources, include the geography and projection period, and label whether a number is an employment projection or a survey expectation. When a title is not directly tracked as an occupation, say so.
